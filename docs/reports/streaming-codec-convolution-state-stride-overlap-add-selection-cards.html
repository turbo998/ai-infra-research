UNDER THE HOOD

Align stride frames before calling the kernel; pad only once at EOS.

SYNTHETIC DEMO · independently_reproduced=false · no GPU/audio-quality measurement

effective_kernel = (K - 1) * dilation + 1
history = effective_kernel - stride
pending += transport_packet
aligned = floor(len(pending) / stride) * stride
emit conv(previous + pending[:aligned])
retain incomplete input, not a new padded chunk

↓ transpose reconstruction

raw = local_transpose(frames) + bias
raw[:K-S] += old_partial
new_partial = raw[-(K-S):] - bias
emit raw[:len(frames) * S]

New stream: clear ingress AND history AND partial.
Alignment matters: this source uses effective-kernel minus stride history, not a universal K−1 convention. K=S is a no-overlap special case in the implementation.

Lifecycle gates

EOS pads one unfinished stride frame, or rejects it explicitly.

Reused slots reset; interleaved streams keep independent buffers.

A finished stream rejects more packets. Transpose final tail stays trimmed.

Chunk partition invariance is an actual test. It does not prove equivalence for replicate padding, arbitrary normalizers, resampling, RVQ or a full neural codec.

MY TAKE

Keep state per stream; use packet boundaries only for transport.

SYNTHETIC DEMO · independently_reproduced=false · no GPU/audio-quality measurement

✅ Good fit

Explicit chunk adapters.

Known padding/alignment convention.

Offline-vs-streamed waveform checks.

Complete state reset on reuse.

⛔ Not for

Resetting history at every packet.

Padding every packet independently.

Emitting a trimmed transpose tail.

Claiming toy parity implies audio quality.
