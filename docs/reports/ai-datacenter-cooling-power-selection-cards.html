Architect Verdict: Good Fit vs. Not For & Production MEP Readiness

Clear architectural guardrails for matching GPU rack thermal density to the right cooling and power delivery tier.

✅ Good Fit (When to Use Each Tier)

• Tier 3 Single-Phase Cold-Plate DLC + CDU (`80–140 kW/rack`): Mandatory for `GB200/GB300 NVL72` and greenfield `H200/B200` clusters. Delivers `PUE 1.12`, `100%` passive copper NVLink backplane (`-$4.48M` optics BOM), `100%` sustained clocks, and `-$6.59M (-46.0%)` 3-yr TCO.

• Tier 2 Rear-Door Heat Exchanger (`40–65 kW/rack`): Ideal brownfield retrofit for `3–4 × HGX H100/H200` per rack where room air conditioning is maxed out and server-level plumbing is not feasible.

• Tier 1 Forced-Air (`≤35 kW/rack`): Small PCIe inference pods (`L40S / H100 NVL`) in standard colocation cages.

⛔ Not For (Anti-Patterns to Avoid)

• Never split a 72-GPU NVL72 domain across 4 air-cooled racks: Exceeding the `~1.5m` copper backplane reach forces expensive, power-hungry inter-rack optical transceivers (`+$280K` and `+4.5 kW` per domain).

• Never feed raw cooling-tower water or sub-dew-point chilled water into GPU cold plates: Always isolate primary `FWS` and secondary `TCS` loops via a CDU with `25 μm`-filtered `PG25` and clamp $T_{\text{supply}} \ge T_{\text{dew}} + 3.0^\circ\text{C}$.

• Never ignore the residual `18%` (`~23 kW/rack`) air heat on cold-plate racks: DIMMs, PSUs, and 800G optics still require CRAH or RDHx airflow.
